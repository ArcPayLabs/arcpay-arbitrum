ArcPay Arbitrum is built around a simple thesis: autonomous agents need a financial control plane before they should be allowed to spend, trade, invoice, or route treasury activity on a live L2.

Arbitrum already has the execution environment, developer tooling, DeFi liquidity, and institutional adoption surface. ArcPay adds the operator layer that sits between agents and money movement.

## Why Arbitrum

- **Arbitrum Sepolia / One** give EVM compatibility and low-cost execution for fast MVP deployment.
- **Stylus** creates a path for policy verification in Rust/WASM while keeping Solidity compatibility.
- **GMX and Arbitrum DeFi** make execution intents and treasury routing relevant beyond a toy demo.
- **ZeroDev-style smart accounts** make agent onboarding and scoped permissions easier to package.
- **Dune analytics** gives public evidence dashboards for audit, adoption, and grant reporting.
- **Robinhood Chain / Orbit path** gives ArcPay an expansion target inside the broader Arbitrum stack.

## What ArcPay Adds

- Agent identity and service discovery.
- x402 paid HTTP resources for agent work.
- Escrow/order state machine for paid tasks.
- Time-window treasury policies.
- Privacy intents for delayed recipient disclosure.
- USDC invoice and spend-card flows.
- Reputation and audit records.
- CLI, MCP, OpenAPI, and hosted developer tools.

## Long-Term Direction

ArcPay should become the treasury and procurement layer for agentic businesses on Arbitrum: agents can request paid tools, operators can enforce spend policies, and every completed action has verifiable evidence.
